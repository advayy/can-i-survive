Important assumptions
  • This is an alpha planning simulator, not financial advice or a prediction of your future.
  • Canada can use a simplified 2026 tax model with federal and provincial brackets plus approximate CPP and EI. It is not a tax-return calculator and does not include every credit, deduction, benefit, or account rule.
  • Baseline uses smoother financial assumptions. Life Events and Monte Carlo can vary markets, inflation, recessions, career events, mortgage renewals, and other shocks.
  • Typical Career combines public wage anchors with modelled career growth, promotions, layoffs, unemployment periods, and re-employment salary changes.
  • Housing adds home equity to net worth, but the simulator does not automatically sell or refinance the home to cover a cash shortfall.
  • The survivability result is mainly a liquidity test: cash and liquid investments can be used when spending exceeds income. A household can have positive total net worth and still run short of spendable money.
  • Named life events and historical stress scenarios are illustrative model inputs. Their timing and cost are not personal forecasts.
  • Canada has the most source-aware defaults right now. Other countries use the same engine but rely more heavily on values you enter yourself.
  • Monte Carlo percentiles and sensitivity bars describe outcomes inside this model. They are not confidence guarantees about the real world.

Alpha

Try out a possible future.

Can I Survive? is a little retirement what-if game. Put in a rough version of your finances and watch one possible life play out.

Change things and run it again. Save more, retire later, buy a home, get laid off, hit a rough market, or simply live longer than you planned for.

This is still an alpha. It mixes public data with editable modelling assumptions, so treat the results as a planning experiment, not financial advice.
